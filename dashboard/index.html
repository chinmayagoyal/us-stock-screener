Method: filter, metrics and flags
Initial filter
Common stock only (no ETFs, funds, preferreds, warrants, units, SPACs) · market cap ≥ $2B · price ≥ $5 and 60-day median dollar volume ≥ $10M · at least 1 year of trading · excludes banks, lenders, brokers, asset managers, insurers and REITs by SEC industry (SIC) code, because they need book-value or FFO models; exchanges and ratings/data firms stay in · SEC US-GAAP financials in USD no older than 200 days · trailing-12-month operating income and operating cash flow both positive · net debt/EBITDA ≤ 4× (or net cash) and EBIT/interest ≥ 3×.
Value score
Cycle-adjusted EBIT/enterprise value (average of trailing 12 months and the last 3 fiscal years, so peak-cycle profits don't look cheap), free-cash-flow yield after stock-based compensation, forward earnings yield, and net shareholder yield (dividends plus the fall in diluted share count).
Quality score
Return on invested capital, gross profit/assets, Piotroski F-score (0–9), accruals (lower is better), net debt/EBITDA (lower is better), 5-year operating-margin volatility (lower is better), revenue growth.
Momentum, revisions, sentiment
12-1 and 6-1 month returns and price vs 200-day average; 90-day change in consensus EPS for this and next fiscal year, latest and 4-quarter average EPS surprise, and 30-day revision breadth; short interest (lower is better) and net insider buying.
Long-term score (1–3 years)
60% value + 40% quality. Flag: LT score ≥ 90, value ≥ 70, F-score ≥ 5, and the price implies at most 5% a year of perpetual free-cash-flow growth. At most 4 names per industry.
Short-term score (1–6 months)
35% momentum + 30% revisions + 20% value + 15% sentiment. Flag: ST score ≥ 90, value ≥ 40, positive 12-1 momentum and revisions ≥ 50. At most 4 names per industry.
Both lists
Cheap, good-quality businesses whose price trend and estimates have already turned up, so they don't rely only on a long wait for the market to re-rate them.
Value-trap risk
Value ≥ 80 and either quality < 30, or momentum < 20 together with revisions < 30: cheap, while the business or the estimates are deteriorating.
Compounders screen
A separate, wider universe: market cap ≥ $500M and ≥ $3M daily trading. Gates: 3-year revenue CAGR ≥ 15%, trailing revenue growth ≥ 10%, revenue up in every one of the last 3 fiscal years, operating profit in each of the last 2, steady operating margins (which excludes commodity booms and one-off licence windfalls), ROIC ≥ 12%, positive operating cash flow, net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3×. Score: 35% growth (revenue CAGR, trailing growth, gross-margin trend), 30% returns (ROIC, ROIC trend, reinvestment rate, margin stability), 20% valuation (EV/EBIT per point of growth, free-cash-flow yield), 15% alignment (insider ownership, smaller size). The top 25 are flagged, at most 4 per industry.
New quality signals
The long-term quality score also rewards an improving ROIC (change over 2 fiscal years); the sentiment score also rewards higher insider ownership.
Implied FCF growth
The perpetual free-cash-flow growth (after stock compensation) the current price implies at a 9% cost of equity, a reverse DCF. Low or negative means the market expects little.
Track record
Each day's lists are entered at the next session's close and compared with the equal-weighted candidate set and SPY. Only lists this tracker actually published are used, so there is no hindsight.
Data
Listings from Nasdaq; fundamentals rebuilt from SEC EDGAR XBRL filings; prices, estimates and short interest from Yahoo Finance (unofficial). XBRL tagging varies, so check any name against its filings before acting. Code and full workbook: github.com/chinmayagoyal/us-stock-screener.
